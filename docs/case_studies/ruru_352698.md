## Public-board review

### Day 2: 3-1-2 initial panda

Claims:

- ずほぬれお化け: 参加できるよ black
- ぴゅりくれあ: 参加できるよ white

The shared pair ordered a grey vote.

The central reason to hold the panda was guard allocation. The wolves know the panda's actual color and can normally exclude at least one false claimant, while the village cannot. Executing the panda makes the next medium result unusually important and pulls guard probability toward the medium. Holding the panda keeps protection concentrated on seer candidates and purchases another result from all three claimants.

Panda execution was still reasonable because it offered high medium information and guard protection from the grey vote. The correct label is a real tradeoff, not a hard rule.

The best grey execution targets were はめたろう and みすりる. Their problem was not message count alone: public information increased without a matching increase in concrete board, claimant, or grey judgment. 涙の数㇠け and ラッキーダガー had higher future information value. サード・アイ and 星見鳥ラリス argued for panda execution, but a debatable progression claim is not wolf evidence when the reasoning is explicit and testable.

## Full-log corrections

### The panda was not a complex joint plan

Before day 2:

- ぴゅり planned to claim and enclose 参加;
- ずぶぬれ independently found 参加 black;
- 参加 had rejected a seer counterclaim;
- ぴゅり misread the initial black as directed at herself and came out.

A complex public structure can be the collision of independent preparation, a true result, and an error.

### Do not over-rationalize attacks

For each attack, separate:

1. public strategic explanations;
2. actual private intent;
3. quality of that intent;
4. accidental result.

The 17Aマン attack accidentally removed the guard. Shared attacks fit a sophisticated body-cut credit battle but were chosen through guard fear, weak coordination, and improvised abnormality.

### Fox claimant and latent madman

バリアント behaved like a serious true claimant: detailed reasons, credit seeking, attack concern, and plausible body-cut analysis. This is a counterexample to classifiers that map active credit work to true or wolf only.

野村 received whites from both surviving claimants and was practically confirmed non-wolf, but was a latent madman. “Confirmed non-wolf” is not “confirmed village faction.” That distinction mattered only near PP/endgame accounting and did not justify early suspicion.

## Reusable lessons

1. **Unusual compositions are stage-dependent.** Keep fox mixing logical in ordinary 3-1 starts, but escalate it when it changes today's action.
2. **Correct wolf identification can lose.** Recalculate wolf count, fox survival, and immediate faction-win conditions before every execution.
3. **A safe non-LW execution can buy a decisive night.**
4. **Ability targets follow the loss condition.** Opponent divination can dominate grey compression.
5. **Attack fit is not attack intent or attack quality.**
6. **Emotional naturalness and sound public arguments are faction-neutral.**
7. **Two-corpse attribution requires current target, prior results, and chronology.**
