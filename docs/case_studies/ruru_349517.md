## Public-board timeline

### Day 2: 2-0, medium absence, one freemason claim

Publicly certain:

- two seer claims, both with white results;
- no medium claim;
- one freemason claim;
- 16 alive, 7 executions remaining;
- 11 public grey players after removing both claimants, both white targets, and the public freemason;
- the normal action is a grey vote.

Operational weighting:

- medium-first is the practical main line, while latent medium remains logically possible;
- true–madman is the main seer composition;
- true–wolf remains a normal alternative;
- fox mixing or a latent true seer behind madman–wolf is recorded but should not distort the day's vote without new evidence.

Important correction:

Under a true–madman assumption, at least three of the three wolves and one fox are in the 11-player grey, but the composition is not fixed to “two wolves plus the fox.” If the madman's white target is the fox, all three wolves remain in the grey.

Speech analysis did not reliably identify all roles. Grasshopper showed “speech construction” around a low-impact hypothetical topic, and Yakushiji repeatedly avoided committing to board or grey evaluations. Those are useful low-resolution signals for hidden information or survival pressure, not reliable wolf/fox/guard classifiers. Yuki and Solanax passed the day with ordinary-looking contributions.

### Night 2: true–madman begel and seer guard

The wolves had no seer truth distinction and chose an immediate candidate attack. The strategic logic:

- a 1/2 role-choice chance to select the true seer before considering guard;
- even a madman death damages trust in the remaining true seer;
- the cost is losing a faction member who could otherwise enclose wolves, issue false black results, consume an execution, or contribute to PP;
- the wolves accept weak active fox management and plan to let votes/designations remove the fox.

The guard protected Madulite. Corona, the madman, died. This is a clean example of why “the attacked seer candidate was true” is invalid.

### Day 3: dead seer candidate and remaining-seer hold

The major public worlds were:

1. Madulite true / corona madman;
2. corona true / Madulite madman;
3. corona true / Madulite wolf.

The attack alone did not distinguish them. Freemason FO removed Madulite's initial-wolf-enclosure world but gave only a weak true-seer update.

The correct operational choice was to retain Madulite rather than execute the surviving candidate immediately, while processing Yakushiji after another day of low-information, self-defensive speech and a no-CO response.

General lesson: an attacked candidate's prior vote is only supporting evidence. The candidate already had a strong role-based reason to be attacked, so “they voted X and then died, therefore X is wolf” is confounded.

### Night 3 and night 4: repeated white-target attacks

The wolves attacked Madulite's whites, first Gravestone and then Kocchi Koi Yo.

The attack is multi-world robust:

- Madulite true: remove true whites, prevent white compression, preserve a large grey;
- Madulite madman: check whether a false white is a fox and remove a hard-to-execute position;
- Madulite wolf: create a self-bolcon appearance, check fox candidates, and leave partners in the grey.

This is better described as “white-compression prevention plus passive fox-enclosure checking” than as pure fox search.

Pink was not in the same board position. Pink was corona's white and remained in Madulite's grey. Attacking Pink would narrow a true Madulite's grey; attacking Madulite's white would not.

### Guard decision: role belief and action utility diverge

Demacia described Madulite as substantially wolf-like yet guarded the candidate again before later moving to Nobori Ryuu.

This is not contradictory. The guard choice protects the world in which Madulite is true because the loss is large, even if that world is not the guard's most probable one. Later, once useful white results existed and the board needed a stable designator, the expected loss shifted toward the freemason.

Store this as a central design rule:

> “Most likely role” and “best action under asymmetric loss” are separate outputs.

### Days 4–5: hypotheses should be converted into tests

The highest-quality public intervention was Demacia's demand:

> If Madulite argues Pink may be a bleach wolf, divine Pink and show the result.

This is stronger than another round of impression debate because it converts a hypothesis into a future observation.

Weak recurring inferences included:

- “not attacked, therefore wolf”;
- “a true–madman board would never leave the remaining true seer alive”;
- “Madulite's whites are attacked, therefore Madulite must be true”;
- “Pink survives, therefore bleach”;
- “a player attacks a true seer's white, therefore the player is fox rather than wolf.”

Each observation had multiple live strategic explanations.

### Day 6: Yuki black

Publicly certain:

- 8 alive, 3 executions;
- Madulite published Yuki black;
- Yuki was also grey from corona's perspective;
- every non-freemason prior Madulite white had become a night corpse;
- Yuki claimed no role.

Executing Yuki was reasonable but not logically safe.

Benefits:

- catches a wolf in the Madulite-true world;
- Yuki had no protection from corona's result;
- no guard exposure;
- preserves another Madulite result.

Risks:

- if Madulite was false, Yuki could be village;
- if Yuki was the final wolf and a fox lived, immediate execution could lose.

Critical correction:

“Grey from the dead true candidate's perspective” means “not protected,” not “safe to execute under every world.”

Solanax's attack on Yuki after the designation/black was nearly decided has low line-clearing value. Low-cost attacks after a partner's execution becomes unavoidable must be discounted.

### Night 6 and day 7: the decisive peace

Solanax attacked Nobori Ryuu. Demacia guarded Nobori Ryuu, producing peace.

The peace changed the execution count from two to three. Its main value was not merely preserving one freemason; it purchased a full controlled night.

After Demacia's single guard claim and Solanax's LW claim, the village could:

1. avoid immediate LW execution;
2. execute PowerPoint;
3. order Madulite to divine Solanax;
4. order Demacia to guard Solanax;
5. interpret the next morning's death pattern.

This is the strongest reusable scene in the case.

The guard instruction does not guarantee “no peace whether the target is wolf or fox.” It removes off-target guard successes. If Solanax were a fox and another wolf attacked Solanax, peace could still occur.

### Day 8: world convergence

Madulite alone died while Solanax survived under guard.

Under the main assumptions:

- if Madulite was true and Solanax was a fox, the completed divination would have killed Solanax too;
- if Madulite was madman, corona was true and Pink was a true white; the two freemasons and near-confirmed guard were also non-wolves, leaving Solanax as the wolf;
- Madulite could not be a wolf or fox killed by an ordinary wolf attack.

The main worlds converged on Solanax wolf. Village executed Solanax and won.

## What this case changed in the project

### 1. Optimize for decisions under uncertainty, not role-label accuracy

Early speech provided weak, role-nonspecific signals. The winning edge came from preserving options and forcing future observations.

Primary system objective:

- reconstruct facts;
- maintain possible worlds;
- weight worlds without converting unusual into impossible;
- compare actions by faction utility, information gain, and worst-case loss.

Role probabilities are intermediate state, not the final product.

### 2. Add an explicit action layer

For every cutoff, store separately:

- role/faction assessment;
- execution value;
- information value;
- endgame judgeability;
- ability-use value;
- loss in the important alternative worlds.

Examples from this game:

- wolf-like but still guard;
- likely villager but execute as a low-information no-CO;
- true-like seer but schedule a deadline execution;
- previously village-read player but execute after hard endgame convergence.

### 3. Grade reasoning quality independently from outcome

A correct role guess with weak reasoning is not a high-quality example. An incorrect conclusion reached through valid, well-calibrated updates may be useful.

Score:

- fact fidelity;
- hard-logic validity;
- alternative-world coverage;
- calibration of strength;
- causal de-duplication;
- action regret;
- information gain;
- counterfactual robustness.

### 4. Read future games in two passes

Pass 1 — playable perspective only:

- freeze cutoff;
- record public/private facts available to the actor;
- enumerate representative worlds;
- rank them qualitatively;
- recommend action and state expected losses;
- record the next observation that would be most useful.

Pass 2 — true-world postmortem:

- reveal roles, private talk, monologues, and ability actions;
- separate genuine signal from hindsight;
- label accidental correctness;
- create counterexamples;
- turn expert corrections into prevention rules.

### 5. Keep thin worlds without letting them drive routine play

Use the labels:

- main line;
- strong alternative;
- thin but operationally relevant;
- logical only;
- impossible.

A logical-only world stays in the record but does not change routine execution order unless the action is cheap or the consequence is catastrophic enough to justify it.
